% -- 1. Introduction --------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}

We study daily \emph{Realized Variance} (RV) for the 30 constituents
of the Dow Jones Industrial Average using high-frequency intraday data
spanning January 2003 to March 2022 ($\approx$4\,800 trading days per
stock). RV is constructed as the sum of squared 5-minute log-returns:
\begin{equation}
  RV_t = \sum_{s=1}^{n} r_s^2, \quad r_s = \log\!\left(\frac{P_s}{P_{s-1}}\right)
\end{equation}
Five-minute sampling balances microstructure noise against information
content. We compare econometric and machine learning (ML) forecasting
models and assess cross-stock predictability via Granger causality.

% -- 2. Data and Descriptive Analysis ---------------------------------
\section{Data and Descriptive Analysis}

Table~\ref{tab:desc} reports descriptive statistics for $\ln RV$ for
four representative stocks. Raw RV is right-skewed and leptokurtic; the
log-transform substantially reduces both. Sample ACFs of $\ln RV$ decay
very slowly, consistent with long memory. Augmented Dickey--Fuller
tests reject the unit-root null for \textbf{all 30 stocks} at the 5\%

% -- 3. Models --------------------------------------------------------
\section{Models}

\paragraph{HAR (Corsi, 2009).}
\begin{equation}
  y_t = \beta_0 + \beta_d y_{t-1}
        + \beta_w \bar{y}^{(5)}_{t-1}
        + \beta_m \bar{y}^{(22)}_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t
  \label{eq:har}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{y}^{(n)}_{t-1}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{t-i}$.
This is a restricted AR(22) estimated by OLS, capturing heterogeneous
trader horizons. HAR coefficients are positive and economically
meaningful across all 30 stocks, with $\beta_d > \beta_w > \beta_m$
on average and $R^2 \approx 0.65$.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} AR(1) and a random walk ($\hat{y}_{t+1}=y_t$).

\paragraph{Machine Learning.}
Four ML models use the same three HAR predictors for a fair comparison:
\textbf{LASSO} (L1-regularised), \textbf{Ridge} (L2-regularised),
\textbf{XGBoost} (gradient-boosted trees), and
\textbf{LightGBM} (histogram-based boosting).
All ML models are retrained annually (every 252 trading days), following
the approach of \cite{kilic2025}.

% -- 4. Forecasting Results -------------------------------------------
\section{Out-of-Sample Forecasting}

Models are evaluated on the last 50\% of observations (horizon $h=1$,
annual retraining). Table~\ref{tab:rmse} reports average RMSE and MFE
across all 30 stocks.

\midrule
Ridge     & \textbf{0.5107} & 0.0147 \\

Key findings: (i) HAR, LASSO, and Ridge are statistically
indistinguishable at the top, confirming the difficulty of beating a
well-specified linear benchmark~\cite{kilic2025}. (ii) Tree-based ML
models (XGBoost, LightGBM) rank fourth and fifth -- competitive but
offering no consistent improvement over HAR. (iii) The Random Walk is
always worst, confirming significant predictability in $\ln RV$.
(iv) All models are positively biased (MFE $>0$), except the Random
Walk which is nearly unbiased.

% -- 5. Granger Causality ---------------------------------------------
\section{Granger Causality}

We test all $30 \times 29 = 870$ directed pairs for Granger causality
using the minimum F-test p-value across lags 1--10.
\textbf{869 out of 870 pairs} (99.9\%) are significant at the 5\%
level, indicating pervasive cross-stock volatility spillovers across
the DJIA. This is consistent with the strong within-index co-movement
documented in the literature.

For the top-10 pairs by significance we run bivariate VAR forecasts
(parameters estimated once on the training set). The VAR improves on
the univariate HAR in \textbf{4 out of 10 cases}, with an average RMSE
reduction of 0.004 for winning pairs. While Granger causality is
statistically ubiquitous, its \emph{incremental} forecasting value is
modest -- consistent with \cite{kilic2025}'s finding that ML and richer
models offer limited gains over parsimonious benchmarks.

% -- 6. Conclusion ----------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}

HAR, LASSO, and Ridge are jointly the best forecasting models for
$\ln RV$ across the DJIA, with near-identical RMSE of 0.51.
Tree-based ML methods are competitive but offer no consistent advantage.
Cross-stock Granger causality is statistically pervasive (869/870 pairs
significant) yet adds limited out-of-sample forecasting value, with VAR
improvements only modest (RMSE $\Delta \approx 0.004$). These results
reinforce the conclusion of \cite{kilic2025}: parsimonious linear
econometric models remain the reliable benchmark for realized variance
forecasting.
